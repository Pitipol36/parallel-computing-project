The problem: millions of ray queries

Every pixel shoots rays into a scanned mesh, and each ray must find its closest (or any) hit among up to a million triangles.

1

primary ray

closest hit: which triangle does this pixel see?

1

shadow ray

any hit toward the light: is the point lit?

N

ambient-occlusion rays

any hit nearby: how enclosed is the point?

12.4M

rays per frame

at 1920×1080 with 4 AO samples

2 · BVH ray tracing on CUDA

Each pixel needs a primary ray to find what it sees, a shadow ray toward the light, and N ambient-occlusion rays that test how enclosed the surface is. At full HD with 4 AO samples that is about 12 million rays per frame. Every ray is independent, so there is a lot of data parallelism. But each ray walks an irregular tree, which brings warp divergence and scattered memory access. That is a different pattern from the labs: not a stencil, scan or reduction, but hierarchical tree traversal.
