CPU-quality trees, 5× faster to build

Renders 16–42 % faster than each builder alone; within ±3 % of the CPU SAH trees. Lower SAH ≠ proportionally faster.

24 · PRBVH after Meister & Bittner 2018. CPU twin identical node for node. Dragon column = total build time incl. 8 rounds.

The last quality step works on any of the GPU trees. Parallel reinsertion takes a finished binary tree and asks, for every node at once: where else in the tree could this node go so that the total SAH cost drops the most? It computes that gain exactly, with a branch-and-bound search. Many moves can be applied together as long as they don't touch the same nodes, so each move locks its six nodes with an atomic max on a priority key, and the winners are applied in parallel. Then the boxes are refitted, and the round is kept only if the cost went down. After eight rounds, PLOC, H-PLOC and TRBVH are within about two percent of the full-sweep reference on the scans, the LBVH within five, and TRBVH plus PRBVH beats it. On the mixed scene all four end up five to fifteen percent lower in SAH cost than the full sweep, because the full sweep is still greedy at the top of the tree. H-PLOC plus PRBVH builds the dragon in 150 milliseconds with a tree as good as the binned CPU builder's by SAH cost, and the CPU takes 770. The price is about 13 to 20 milliseconds per round, mostly the search. Compared with each builder alone, renders get 16 to 42 percent faster, so the extra time pays off after about 11 to 50 frames. One caution: SAH cost is a model. Against the CPU SAH trees themselves, the PRBVH trees render within about 3 percent: slightly faster on the mixed scene, slightly slower on the dragon, where the full-sweep tree is still the fastest. A 15 percent lower SAH cost bought only 9 percent fewer box tests and a 2 to 5 percent faster render. So the big render gains come from fixing weak trees.
