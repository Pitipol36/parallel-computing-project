Sequential solution: a SAH BVH

A bounding volume hierarchy nests boxes around groups of triangles. A ray skips every box it misses, so it tests only a few dozen triangles instead of all of them.

The Surface Area Heuristic picks each split to minimise the expected traversal cost. Binning (16 bins per axis) keeps the build at O(T log T).

brute force

O(P · T)

with a BVH (P rays, T triangles)

O(P log T) + O(T log T)

Measured on the bunny at 320×240: brute force on the GPU takes 3.6 s; the BVH on one CPU core takes 38 ms.

4 · BVH ray tracing on CUDA

The sequential algorithm is a bounding volume hierarchy built with the Surface Area Heuristic. Brute force costs rays times triangles. A BVH brings each ray down to about log T, plus a one-off T log T build. SAH is the standard quality measure used in PBRT and the literature, and binning gives near full-SAH quality while keeping the build fast. The number at the bottom is the key point: brute force on the GPU is a hundred times slower than the BVH on a single CPU core. Algorithm first, then parallelism.
