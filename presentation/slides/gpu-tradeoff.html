The speed has a price. A Morton curve sorts triangles by position only and never looks at surface area, so the tree is looser: its SAH cost is 21 to 30 percent higher than the SAH builder's, rays test about one and a half times as many triangles, and rendering is 25 to 32 percent slower. The SAH cost predicted the measured traversal counts well. Whether that matters depends on how many rays one tree serves. Total time to image is build plus render, and on the dragon the SAH tree only catches up after about 5.8 billion rays, roughly 470 full-HD frames with 4 AO samples. For anything less, the fast build wins.
