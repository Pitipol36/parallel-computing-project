The speed has a price. A Morton curve sorts triangles by position only and never looks at surface area, so the tree is looser: on the scanned models its SAH cost is 19 to 30 percent above the full-sweep SAH reference, rays do about 20 to 25 percent more box tests, and rendering is 10 to 24 percent slower. On the mixed-size scene it is far worse: 82 percent above the reference and 52 percent slower to render. I use the single-threaded CPU render time here because the GPU kernel times vary too much with background load. Whether the looser tree matters depends on how many rays one tree serves. Total time to image is build plus render, and on the dragon the SAH tree only catches up after about 5.2 billion rays, roughly 415 full-HD frames with 4 AO samples. For anything less, the fast build wins.
