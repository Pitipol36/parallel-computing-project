The speed has a price. A Morton curve sorts triangles by position only and never looks at surface area, so the tree is looser: its SAH cost is 18 to 28 percent higher than the SAH builder's, rays do about 20 percent more box tests on the big models, and rendering is 10 to 19 percent slower. I use the single-threaded CPU render time here because the GPU kernel times vary too much with background load. Whether the looser tree matters depends on how many rays one tree serves. Total time to image is build plus render, and on the dragon the SAH tree only catches up after about 6.8 billion rays, roughly 550 full-HD frames with 4 AO samples. For anything less, the fast build wins.
